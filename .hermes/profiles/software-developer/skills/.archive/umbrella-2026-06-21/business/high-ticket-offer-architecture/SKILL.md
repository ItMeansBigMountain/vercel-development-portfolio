# High-Ticket Offer Architecture Interviews

Use this skill when the user wants to discover, package, position, validate, or price a $3,000+ digital offer such as a course, coaching program, done-with-you service, consulting package, or paid community.

## Core rule: interview, do not lecture

Run a structured interview. Do not jump straight to the offer until the user's lived experience, transformation, audience, and proof are clear.

For this user, keep the interview **laconic and paced**:

- Ask 1–3 questions at a time, not long questionnaires.
- Reflect back only the strongest signal in short bullets.
- Move stage-by-stage and explicitly say what stage you are in.
- If the user says the replies are too wordy, immediately shorten and reduce question count.
- Avoid over-polished marketing language while they are still telling raw story.
- When the user says they will continue a personal story, switch into **story collection mode**: acknowledge, save durable themes if appropriate, ask only a small continuation prompt, and postpone offer extraction until the story arc lands.

## Six-stage interview sequence

### Stage 1 — Background discovery

Goal: uncover the story and emotional/identity stakes.

Ask about:

- personal origin story and major life chapters
- biggest challenges overcome
- career and work path
- unique perspectives earned through lived experience
- what people already ask them for advice on
- proof moments where they helped someone

Do not advance until the core transformation arc is legible.

### Stage 2 — Skill extraction

Goal: identify every monetizable skill or transformation.

Probe for:

- results they created for themselves
- results they created for others
- systems they built without formal permission
- repeated advice that works
- skills they can teach, guide, audit, or implement
- identity shifts they can help others make

Separate skills into: teachable knowledge, coaching insight, technical execution, accountability, network/context, and story-based authority.

### Stage 3 — Market alignment

Goal: identify who would pay for the transformation.

Ask who they naturally understand and who has urgent pain plus buying power. Compare markets by pain intensity, ability to pay, trust fit, and measurable ROI.

### Stage 4 — Offer architecture

Goal: shape the offer format and promise.

Decide between:

- 1:1 coaching
- group coaching
- done-with-you implementation
- done-for-you service
- paid community
- cohort course
- consulting package
- hybrid program

Define promise, duration, weekly cadence, deliverables, access, curriculum/modules, and support boundaries.

### Stage 5 — High-ticket validation

Goal: justify $3k+ pricing.

Validate via:

- measurable transformation
- financial ROI or career ROI
- saved time
- avoided pain/risk
- identity/status upgrade
- access/accountability intensity
- buyer urgency and willingness to pay

If ROI is weak, adjust audience or format before forcing a high price.

### Stage 6 — Positioning and launch path

Goal: package the offer into a clear market message.

Produce:

- offer name options
- one-sentence promise
- target buyer
- mechanism/framework
- guarantee or risk reversal if appropriate
- proof assets needed
- first sales channel
- first 10 outreach/test conversations

## Content-led leverage path

When the user is not ready to sell yet, or the offer depends heavily on lived authority, build a content proof engine before forcing the $3k+ package.

Default ladder:

```text
public transformation log
|
V
audience trust + repeated questions
|
V
free lead magnet / proof asset
|
V
low-ticket starter kit or workshop
|
V
group cohort / community
|
V
$3k+ coaching, consulting, or done-with-you program
```

Use this when the user wants YouTube/TikTok/Instagram to work hand-in-hand with the high-ticket offer. Treat the channel as a validation engine: recurring comments, DMs, and viewer questions reveal the market language and strongest paid promise.

## Lived-experience offer extraction pattern

When the user's story is complex, do not flatten it into one generic niche too early. Track multiple transformation threads, then pick the best monetizable bridge.

Common thread types:

- identity reconstruction
- career escape / career upgrade
- health and discipline
- addiction or overstimulation recovery
- family/cultural conflict
- self-education without formal credentials
- money and mobility
- mentorship/accountability gaps

Look for the bridge between **what the user survived** and **what the market will pay to solve**.

## Pitfalls

- Do not ask a 10-question intake dump after the user has already given rich story.
- Do not turn trauma into a product before confirming the user wants to serve that audience.
- Do not assume the most emotional story is the best-paying market.
- Do not position a high-ticket offer around vague inspiration; anchor it to measurable outcomes.
- Do not skip validation just because the story is compelling.
- Do not invent credentials or outcomes; ask for real proof.
